How to read these results

Prime factorisation breaks a number into the primes that multiply to make it — every whole number above 1 has exactly one such factorisation. Divisors are all numbers that divide it evenly; their sum tells you whether a number is abundant (sum of proper divisors bigger than the number), deficient (smaller) or perfect (equal).

The digital root repeatedly sums digits to a single digit and equals the remainder after dividing by 9 (with 9 instead of 0) — a classic quick check for arithmetic errors. Representations show the same value in binary, octal, hexadecimal and base 36, which is useful for developers, and the number in words for cheques and contracts.

Date readings are deliberately mechanical: spreadsheets often turn IDs and invoice numbers into dates (and vice versa). If a number shows up as a strange date in your files, these readings help you spot what happened.

FAQ

How large a number can I analyze?

Any whole number from 1 to 999,999,999,999. Factorisation uses trial division, which is instant at this size.

What is a sphenic number?

A number that is the product of three distinct primes, such as 17381 = 7 × 13 × 191.

How are the date readings calculated?

We read the digits as day/month/year, count days from 1 January 1970 (Unix) and use the spreadsheet serial-date system, where day 1 is 1 January 1900.
