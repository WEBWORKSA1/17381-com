Margin vs markup: the pricing mistake that quietly costs you thousands

Why a 50% markup is only a 33% margin, how to convert between them, and a simple pricing table you can use today.

By the 17381 Numbers Lab editorial team · Updated 2026-11-15 · How we write

Margin and markup both describe profit, but against different bases. Mix them up and you underprice every product you sell.

The definitions

  • Markup = profit ÷ cost
  • Margin = profit ÷ price

A product that costs $60 and sells for $90 has $30 profit: a 50% markup, but a 33.3% margin.

Pricing to a target margin

Decide the margin your business model needs, then price with: Price = Cost ÷ (1 − margin). For a 40% margin on a $60 cost: 60 ÷ 0.6 = $100.

Why it matters for discounts

At a 33% margin, a 10% discount removes 30% of your profit per unit. You would need to sell roughly 43% more units to earn the same gross profit. Know that before running a sale.

Checklist

  1. Use landed cost (including freight, fees and packaging).
  2. Set a target margin per product category.
  3. Price from margin, then sanity-check against competitors and perceived value.
  4. Review quarterly — costs drift upward quietly.
