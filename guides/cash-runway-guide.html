Cash runway: how to calculate it, extend it and never be surprised by it

Burn rate, runway, default-alive and a practical plan to extend runway without killing growth.

By the 17381 Numbers Lab editorial team · Updated 2026-11-15 · How we write

Profit is an opinion; cash is a fact. Runway — the number of months until your cash reaches zero — is the fact that matters most for any business that is not yet profitable.

The basic math

Net burn = monthly expenses − monthly revenue. Runway = cash ÷ net burn. With $240,000 in the bank and a $20,000 net burn you have 12 months.

Why the basic math lies

Revenue grows (or shrinks), hires start mid-quarter, annual bills land in one month and customers pay late. Model runway month by month instead of with a single division. Our runway calculator includes revenue growth for exactly this reason.

Default alive or default dead?

Ask: if nothing changes — same growth, same spending — do we reach profitability before the money runs out? If yes, you are default alive and can choose when to raise. If no, the clock is running and every month of delay narrows your options.

Seven ways to extend runway

  1. Collect faster: deposits, upfront annual plans, shorter payment terms.
  2. Pay slower (ethically): negotiate supplier terms.
  3. Cut zombie subscriptions — audit every recurring charge.
  4. Raise prices for new customers first.
  5. Pause channels with CAC payback above 12 months.
  6. Convert fixed costs to variable (freelancers, usage-based tools).
  7. Use non-dilutive financing only where the asset pays the loan back.
Start a fundraise or a cost plan with at least six to nine months of runway left. Decisions made with two months of cash are rarely good ones.
