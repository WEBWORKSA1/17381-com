The 10 numbers every small business owner should know by heart

Revenue is a vanity metric. These ten numbers — from gross margin to runway — tell you whether your business is actually healthy.

By the 17381 Numbers Lab editorial team · Updated 2026-11-15 · How we write

Most owners can quote last month's revenue. Far fewer can quote the numbers that decide whether that revenue turns into cash. Here are the ten worth memorising, in the order they usually matter.

1. Gross margin

Revenue minus the direct cost of delivering it, as a percentage of revenue. It is the budget you have for everything else: marketing, rent, salaries and profit. If gross margin is thin, no amount of volume fixes it. Calculate yours.

2. Contribution margin per unit

Price minus variable cost. It tells you how much each sale chips away at fixed costs, and it drives break-even.

3. Break-even point

Fixed costs divided by contribution margin. Expressed as units per day it becomes a target the whole team can feel. Find your break-even.

4. Net burn and runway

If you are not yet profitable, cash runway is the clock you are playing against. Know it in months, and re-check it every time you hire. Runway calculator.

5. Customer acquisition cost (CAC)

Total sales and marketing spend divided by new customers won. Track it by channel — blended CAC hides the channel that is quietly losing money.

6. Customer lifetime value (LTV)

The gross profit an average customer generates before leaving. Compare it with CAC: a ratio of about 3:1 is a widely used benchmark. LTV:CAC calculator.

7. Churn or repeat rate

Subscription businesses track monthly churn; shops and restaurants track repeat purchase rate. Retention compounds — small improvements change LTV dramatically.

8. Average order value (AOV)

Revenue divided by number of orders. Bundles, upsells and minimum-order thresholds raise AOV without raising CAC.

9. Cash conversion cycle

How many days pass between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. A profitable business can still run out of cash if customers pay in 60 days and suppliers want 15.

10. Revenue per employee

A blunt but useful efficiency check, especially before hiring. Compare it with businesses of a similar model, not across industries.

Start here: write these ten numbers on one page today, even if some are rough guesses. The act of estimating shows you which ones you are flying blind on.
