CAGR vs average growth: why your growth chart might be lying

How compounding distorts simple averages, how to calculate CAGR correctly and when to use each measure.

By the 17381 Numbers Lab editorial team · Updated 2026-11-15 · How we write

Suppose revenue rises 100% in year one and falls 50% in year two. The simple average growth is +25% a year. The actual result? You are exactly back where you started. That is why compound annual growth rate (CAGR) exists.

The formula

CAGR = (Ending value ÷ Starting value)1/years − 1

In the example: (1 ÷ 1)1/2 − 1 = 0%. Honest.

When to use CAGR

  • Comparing investments or business units over multi-year periods.
  • Describing revenue or user growth in a pitch deck.
  • Projecting a steady-state future value.

When CAGR is not enough

CAGR hides the ride. Two companies can share a 20% CAGR while one grew smoothly and the other nearly died in year two. Show the yearly figures alongside it, and consider volatility for investments.

The rule of 72

Divide 72 by the growth rate to estimate doubling time: 12% doubles in about 6 years, 24% in about 3. It is a fast way to make growth rates intuitive for a team or investor.

Monthly to annual

A 5% monthly growth rate is not 60% a year — it compounds to (1.05)12 − 1 ≈ 79.6%.
