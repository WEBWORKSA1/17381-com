Lucky and unlucky numbers in global commerce

How 8, 4, 7, 13 and other numbers influence pricing, real estate, phone numbers and domain names across cultures.

By the 17381 Numbers Lab editorial team · Updated 2026-11-15 · How we write

Numbers carry cultural meaning, and that meaning shows up in prices, floor plans, licence plates and launch dates. If you sell internationally, it is worth knowing the basics.

8 — prosperity (Chinese-speaking markets)

In Mandarin and Cantonese, the word for eight sounds similar to words associated with wealth and prosperity. Prices, phone numbers and addresses with 8s can be considered desirable. The Beijing Olympics famously opened on 08/08/08 at 8:08 pm.

4 — avoided in parts of East Asia

In Chinese, Japanese and Korean, four sounds similar to the word for death. Some buildings skip fourth floors, and products are rarely sold in sets of four in those markets.

13 — avoided in many Western contexts

Many hotels and towers skip a 13th floor or relabel it, and some airlines omit row 13.

7 — lucky in many Western cultures

Seven appears in slot machines, brand names and marketing as a lucky or "complete" number.

9 — long-lasting

In Chinese, nine sounds like the word for "long-lasting", which makes it popular for weddings and anniversaries.

What this means for numeric brands and domains

Short numeric names travel across languages without translation, which is one reason five-digit numeric .com names (there are only 100,000 of them) attract interest. Digit composition — more 8s and 6s, fewer 4s — can influence how certain audiences perceive them. Our own name, 17381, contains an 8 and no 4; read more in the story of 17381.

Practical tips

  • Research number associations before pricing, packaging or naming for a new market.
  • Avoid sets or price endings that carry negative associations locally.
  • Do not overdo it — value and clarity still matter more than numerology.
