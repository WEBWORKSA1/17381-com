How it works

Total ROI compares what you gained with what you invested. Annualised ROI converts that into an equivalent yearly rate using compounding, so you can compare a 3-year marketing project with a 1-year equipment purchase or an index fund.

The formulas

  • Net gain = Final value − Investment
  • ROI % = Net gain ÷ Investment × 100
  • Annualised ROI = (Final value ÷ Investment)1/years − 1

Worked example

$10,000 becomes $16,500 after 3 years. Net gain is $6,500, total ROI 65%, but annualised ROI is (1.65)1/3 − 1 = 18.2% per year. Divide 65% by 3 and you would wrongly claim 21.7%.

Making ROI honest

  • Include all costs: setup, your own time, tools, fees and taxes.
  • Use cash actually received, not projected revenue.
  • Compare with a benchmark — the return you would get from paying down debt or a low-risk alternative.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good ROI?

It depends on risk and time. Compare annualised ROI with your cost of capital and with low-risk alternatives available to you.

Is ROI the same as profit margin?

No. Margin relates profit to revenue; ROI relates profit to the money invested.

Can ROI be negative?

Yes — if you get back less than you put in, ROI is below zero.
