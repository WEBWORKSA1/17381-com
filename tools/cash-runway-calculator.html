How it works

Net burn is expenses minus revenue. Simple runway divides cash by burn. This calculator goes further: it simulates month by month, growing revenue at your chosen rate, so you can see whether growth closes the gap before the money runs out.

The formulas

  • Net burn = Monthly expenses − Monthly revenue
  • Simple runway = Cash ÷ Net burn
  • Growth-adjusted runway: repeat cash = cash + revenue − expenses, with revenue × (1 + growth) each month

Worked example

$180,000 cash, $22,000 revenue and $38,000 expenses gives a $16,000 burn — about 11 months of simple runway. At 2% monthly revenue growth burn shrinks every month, which stretches runway from about 11 to about 14 months — but cash still runs out before revenue catches up with expenses. That is the gap a raise, a price change or cost cuts must close.

What healthy looks like

  • Start fundraising or cost cuts with at least 6–9 months of runway left — deals take time.
  • Track default alive vs. default dead: at current growth and spend, do you reach profitability before cash runs out?
  • Build a "cut plan" in advance: what would you stop on day one if revenue dipped?

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between gross and net burn?

Gross burn is total monthly spending; net burn subtracts revenue. Runway uses net burn.

How much runway should a startup have?

Many founders aim for 12–24 months after a raise, and start the next process with 6–9 months remaining.
