How it works

CAGR asks: if something grew at the same rate every year, what rate would take it from the starting value to the ending value in the time given? It ignores the path and only uses the endpoints.

The formula

CAGR = (End ÷ Start)1 ÷ years − 1

Worked example

Revenue grew from $120,000 to $310,000 in 4 years. CAGR = (310,000 ÷ 120,000)0.25 − 1 = 26.8%. The simple average of total growth (158% ÷ 4 = 39.6%) overstates performance by almost 13 points.

Rule of 72

Divide 72 by the growth rate to estimate doubling time. At 26.8% a year, revenue doubles roughly every 2.7 years. The calculator also shows the exact figure using logarithms.

Limits

  • CAGR hides volatility — a year of −40% and a year of +80% can show the same CAGR as two steady years.
  • It needs positive start and end values.
  • Do not mix nominal and inflation-adjusted figures.

Frequently asked questions

Why not just average yearly growth rates?

Averages ignore compounding and overstate results when growth is uneven. CAGR is the rate that actually reproduces the ending value.

Can I use CAGR for monthly data?

Yes — use months as the period, then convert: annual = (1 + monthly)^12 − 1.
