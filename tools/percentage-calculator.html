The three formulas

  • Percent of: X% of Y = X ÷ 100 × Y
  • What percent: X is (X ÷ Y × 100)% of Y
  • Percentage change: (New − Old) ÷ |Old| × 100

Worked examples

18% sales tax on $2,450 is $441. 340 sign-ups out of 1,700 visitors is a 20% conversion rate. Going from 17,381 to 21,504 is a 23.7% increase (21,504 happens to be the sum of the divisors of 17,381).

Percentage points vs percent

If a conversion rate moves from 2% to 3%, that is a rise of 1 percentage point but a 50% increase. Mixing the two is one of the most common errors in reports and headlines.

Why a 50% loss needs a 100% gain

Percentage changes are not symmetric. Drop from 100 to 50 (−50%) and you need +100% to get back to 100. That asymmetry is why protecting against big losses matters so much in business and investing.

Frequently asked questions

How do I reverse a percentage increase?

Divide by (1 + rate). A price of $118 including 18% tax was $100 before tax.

What is a percentage point?

The simple difference between two percentages, e.g. 5% to 7% is +2 percentage points.
