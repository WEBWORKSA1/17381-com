How it works

Lifetime value (LTV) estimates the gross profit a customer generates before they leave. Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is what you spend in sales and marketing to win one customer. If LTV is not comfortably above CAC, growth simply burns cash faster.

The formulas

  • Gross profit per month = ARPA × Gross margin
  • Average lifetime (months) = 1 ÷ Monthly churn
  • LTV = Gross profit per month × Lifetime
  • LTV:CAC = LTV ÷ CAC
  • CAC payback (months) = CAC ÷ Gross profit per month

Worked example

$79 ARPA at 80% margin is $63.20 of monthly gross profit. With 3% churn the average customer stays 33 months, so LTV ≈ $2,107. Against a $450 CAC that is an LTV:CAC of 4.7:1 and a payback of about 7 months — healthy by common benchmarks.

Levers ranked by impact

  1. Churn — cutting churn from 3% to 2% raises LTV by 50%.
  2. Price / ARPA — flows straight into gross profit.
  3. CAC — channel mix, conversion rate and sales cycle length.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good LTV:CAC ratio?

A common benchmark is 3:1 or better, with CAC payback under 12 months. Much higher can mean you are under-investing in growth.

Should LTV use revenue or gross profit?

Gross profit is more conservative and is what most investors expect.
