How it works

An amortising loan has a fixed payment. Early payments are mostly interest because the balance is high; as the balance shrinks, more of each payment goes to principal. The schedule below the calculator shows that shift year by year.

The formula

Payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n) where P is the amount borrowed, r the monthly rate (annual ÷ 12) and n the number of monthly payments.

Worked example

$50,000 at 8.5% for 5 years costs about $1,026 a month and roughly $11,550 of interest in total. Stretch it to 7 years and the payment drops to about $792 — but total interest rises to around $16,500.

Ways to pay less

  • Compare the APR, not just the rate — fees count.
  • Shorter terms cost more monthly but far less overall.
  • Extra principal payments early in the loan save the most interest (check for prepayment penalties).
  • For business loans, compare the payment with your monthly contribution margin — can the asset pay for itself?

Frequently asked questions

Does this include fees or insurance?

No. Add origination fees to the amount or compare APRs to see the full cost.

What is amortisation?

Spreading a loan into equal payments that cover interest and gradually repay the principal until the balance reaches zero.
