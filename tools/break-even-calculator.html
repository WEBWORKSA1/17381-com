How it works

Every unit you sell contributes price minus variable cost towards your fixed costs. That contribution margin is the engine of break-even: divide your fixed costs by it and you get the number of units needed to cover rent, salaries, software and everything else that does not change with volume.

The formulas

  • Contribution margin (CM) = Price − Variable cost per unit
  • CM ratio = CM ÷ Price
  • Break-even units = Fixed costs ÷ CM
  • Break-even revenue = Fixed costs ÷ CM ratio
  • Units for a target profit = (Fixed costs + Target profit) ÷ CM

Worked example

A studio has $12,000 of monthly fixed costs and sells a $49 product that costs $19 to deliver. CM = $30, so break-even is 12,000 ÷ 30 = 400 units a month — about 13 a day. To earn $5,000 profit it needs (12,000 + 5,000) ÷ 30 = 567 units.

Use it to test decisions

  • Raise price $5: CM becomes $35 and break-even falls to 343 units (−14%).
  • Add a $4,000/month hire: break-even rises to 534 units. Can the hire add 134+ sales?
  • Cut variable cost $3: often easier than finding 40 new customers.
Watch out: if price is at or below variable cost, you can never break even — more volume only loses more money.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as a fixed cost?

Costs that do not change with sales volume in the short term: rent, salaries, insurance, software subscriptions, loan payments.

Why round break-even units up?

You cannot sell a fraction of a unit, so 399.4 units means you need 400 to stop losing money.

How do I calculate break-even for several products?

Use a weighted-average contribution margin based on your sales mix, then divide fixed costs by it.
