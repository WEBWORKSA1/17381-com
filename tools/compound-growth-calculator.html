How it works

Each year your contributions are added and the whole balance grows at the chosen rate, compounded the number of times per year you set. Early on most of the balance is your own money; later, growth on past growth takes over — that crossover is what makes starting early so powerful.

The core formula

For a single lump sum: FV = P × (1 + r/n)n × t, where P is principal, r the annual rate, n compounding periods per year and t years. With regular contributions the calculator adds each month's deposit and compounds the running balance.

Worked example

$5,000 plus $300 a month at 7% for 15 years grows to roughly $113,000: you contribute $59,000 and compounding adds about $54,000. Double the time and growth becomes the majority of the balance.

Use it for business too

Replace "interest" with the return on reinvested profit — for example, what happens if you reinvest $2,000 of monthly profit into marketing that returns 15% a year.

Rates are assumptions, not promises. Markets fall as well as rise; use conservative numbers and compare several scenarios.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I compound?

Use what your account or investment actually does — monthly (12) for most savings accounts, daily (365) for some. The difference is small at typical rates.

What is the rule of 72?

Divide 72 by the annual rate to estimate the years needed to double money. At 8%, about 9 years.
